A new game needs three things: a scenario to play on, a country to play as, and a difficulty.

![The Scenarios tab](/wiki/img/new-game-scenarios.jpg)
*The Scenarios tab. Modern Day is the only built-in one; everything else comes from the Community Hub.*

## Scenarios

A **scenario** is a world — its map, its borders, who owns what, the starting date, which troop
types exist, and any house rules the author wrote. A **game** is one playthrough of a scenario.
One scenario can seed as many games as you like, and playing one never changes it.

**Modern Day** is the only scenario built into the game. Everything else lives on the
[Community Hub](/wiki/community-hub/), including the official presets:

| Scenario | Start |
|---|---|
| World War II | 1939 |
| Medieval | 1200 AD |
| Rome | 117 AD |
| Mongol World | 1300 AD |
| New World | 1650 |
| Bronze Age | 1200 BC |

Open the **Community** tab in the main menu, find one, and hit **Import & Play**. It downloads
into your library and behaves exactly like a built-in scenario from then on.

**Modern Day is played on the real world's provinces** — 3,662 regions, 202 countries with their
own colours and common English names, about 2,500 cities — starting on 1 January 2016, with the
United States as the default country. Campaigns begun on the earlier hand-drawn map are not lost:
they move to a copy called **Modern Day (classic map)** and carry on there, on the map they were
started on.

If a scenario you imported gets updated by its author, an **Update** button appears on its
card. Updating is safe — games already in progress keep working on the version they started on.

## Choosing a country

Selecting **New Game** on a scenario opens the picker, drawn on that scenario's own map. It has
two tabs, **Pick a country** and **Create a faction** (on beta, **Pick a country** and
**Play as a group**).

### Pick a country
![The country picker](/wiki/img/new-game-country-picker.jpg)
*Pick a country, or switch to Create a faction to invent one.*


A map and a searchable list of every country in the scenario. Click one on the map or pick it
from the list — or press **Scenario default** to play whoever the scenario's author chose.

The list is built from the scenario's actual roster, which includes **countries that hold no
land** — governments in exile, movements, organisations. Playing one of those is a very
different game: you have no territory to defend and no territory to lose.

<p class="beta-note"><b>On beta the picker is larger:</b> the map beside a list of the countries
you can play, each with its flag where one is known and its colour on the map, both in the
scenario's own colours. Pointing at a row lights its country on the map.</p>

### Create a faction

This is where you invent a country that does not exist. You give it:

- **A name** — this is its identity everywhere in the game, so choose it properly.
- **A colour** — how it paints on the map.
- **A flag** — optional; pick from the library or leave it blank.
- **Lore** — who this power is: its history, cause and ambitions. The world reads this. It is
  worth writing something real, because it steers the story the AI tells and how every other
  country treats you.
- **Starting territory** — **Claim regions** on the map, or **Start landless**.

The regions you take are carved out of whoever held them, and that country will notice.

### Play as a group

<p class="beta-note"><b>Beta channel only.</b></p>

On beta the picker's second tab is **Play as a group**, and Create a faction is part of it. A
group is something that is not a country — a cartel, a militia, a movement, an outbreak. It owns
no land: it controls an area, and the regions in it stay their countries'.

- **This scenario's groups** lists the groups the scenario already has, each with how many
  regions it controls. Pick one to lead it.
- **Or make a new group** with the same form as a faction: a name, a colour, a flag, a
  **Description** — what the group is and wants, which the AI reads — and a **Starting area**:
  **Choose its area** on the map, or **No area yet**, to take one as the story goes.

The AI then treats you as the group's leader, not a state's. The group cannot sign for a country,
be annexed like one or govern one, and the governments and people where it operates deal with it
as what it is — they may hunt it, bargain with it, use it or ignore it. Ground it gains or loses is
its area growing or shrinking, and the world can push it back but never dissolve or rename it. In a
scenario with its **Groups** feature switched off there is no list, and the same form invents a
new power instead, as Create a faction does.

## Difficulty
![The difficulty grid](/wiki/img/new-game-difficulty.jpg)
*Six levels. Medium is the honest one.*


Six levels. Difficulty does not give the AI secret information or extra resources, and at no
level is the world biased against you — it changes how much **benefit of the doubt** your plans
get, how competent your rivals are, how hard consequences press, and how firm other governments
are at the table. It has its own instructions for the time skip, for diplomacy and for
interactive events.

| | | Your plans | Rivals | Consequences | At the table |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 😴 | **Very Easy** | Very generous reading | Relaxed | Low | Soft |
| 🙂 | **Easy** | Generous | Moderate | Low–medium | Flexible |
| ⚖️ | **Medium** | Neutral | Normal | Normal | Interest-based |
| 😰 | **Hard** | Little leeway | High | High | Firm |
| 🔥 | **Very Hard** | Almost none | Very high | Very high | Very firm |
| 💀 | **Impossible** | None | Elite | Maximum | Maximum |

At the easy end, when several outcomes are equally plausible the less punishing one is preferred
— but no level grants a success the world state makes impossible. At the hard end, weak
assumptions are punished and errors can compound naturally — but even Impossible is "full causal
consequences", never a scripted conspiracy against you.

**Medium** — "the simulator neither helps nor targets the player" — is the honest one and the
right default. The difference between Medium and Hard is
mostly whether a vague order gets a charitable reading — on Hard, *"improve the economy"* will
get you an event about a policy that did not work.

<p class="beta-note"><b>On beta, difficulty decides how things tend to go for you.</b> The levels
no longer only change how strictly your plans are read. The grid says so, from <b>Very Easy</b>,
"Almost everything goes your way", through <b>Medium</b>, where things happen realistically, to
<b>Impossible</b>, "Almost everything goes against you", with the levels between as steps on that
ladder. What it still never does: grant what the world makes impossible, give a reverse with no
cause the story states, or ignore an order — the attempt is always made and always gets an
outcome. On the <a href="/wiki/war-room/#difficulty">battlefields</a> it is part of the fighting itself: the
armies fighting you fight at 85% of their strength on Very Easy and 175% on Impossible, advance
at 90% and 140% of their pace, and draw 70% and 300% of the usual reserves, while Medium is equal
terms. It also sets who commands them: a plainer commander on Very Easy and Easy, and on Hard and
above one that also weighs how you are likely to answer.</p>

Difficulty is set when you start and stored with the game, but you can change it mid-campaign
from the [cheats panel](/wiki/cheats/).

## Opening a campaign

Starting or resuming a game shows its scenario's cover, then a loading screen — the logo turning
over a dark ground — while the map works out every country's surface. A pause here is normal
rather than a hang; the map is revealed once it is drawn.

On a new game's first turn, a scenario with a *World Before Round One* briefing also writes its
own backstory — the recent history that led to the situation you have inherited.

<p class="beta-note"><b>On beta nothing loads until you press Play.</b> The game starts on the
main menu, and a campaign opens only when you press <b>Play</b> on its card. The first time the
game opens after an update, a steel vault door stands in front of the menu with the Open Historia
mark as its wheel: click it and the door opens, onto a notice of what the update brings when there
is one. <b>Show the vault door every time</b>, in Settings → General → Display, shows it at every
start, and with Reduce motion on it simply fades away.</p>

## What gets created

Starting a game copies the scenario's world into a new playthrough and gives it its own set of
documents — world state, game state, events, actions and chat history. From that point the
game is independent: editing the scenario later does not touch it.

Games appear in the **Games** tab of the main menu, under **Last Played** and **Most Played**,
with your country, date, round, and pending actions and events on the card. **Play** opens one
(the game you have open reads **Current**), **Archive** puts it away, and the card's **⋮** holds
Edit, Clone and Export. See [saves and rollback](/wiki/saves/).

<p class="beta-note"><b>On beta the Games tab has Continue Playing and All Games.</b>
<b>Continue Playing</b> is your latest few; <b>All Games</b> is every campaign once, with a search,
<b>Active</b>, <b>All</b> and <b>Archived</b>, and a sort by recently played, most turns, most
played or name. An archived game's card has <b>Delete</b>, and a deleted game waits on the
<b>Recently deleted</b> shelf below, where it can be restored. See
<a href="/wiki/saves/#archiving-and-deleting">archiving and deleting</a>.</p>

## Starting well

- **Take a country you know something about.** You will write better orders for a situation you
  can already picture.
- **Modern Day first.** It is the most polished and the most legible.
- **Medium first.** Very Hard is not more interesting on turn one, just less forgiving.
- **Write the faction lore if you invent a country.** A faction with no lore is a blank to the
  world, and it will be treated like one.
